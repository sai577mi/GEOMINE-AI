      What runs where

      REAL The shortfall model (XGBoost, 50 trees) and the what-if simulator (XGBoost, 300 trees) are trained models, exported to this page and run in your browser. On the 2025 hold-out year the shortfall model scored AUC 0.84; when it said High, a shortfall followed 87% of the time. Both numbers come from simulated production data.

      REAL Sentinel-2 satellite analysis (indices, prospectivity map, seasonal persistence) was run in Python on real imagery of the Munsar area and is presented in the project report.

      SIMULATED Equipment sensors, production history, absenteeism, blasting delays and the 3D drill-hole assays. This page generates them so the full system can be demonstrated. They are replaced by MOIL's SCADA, production and drill databases without changing the models' inputs.

      SIMULATED Rainfall. A seasonal pattern is generated here. In the Python version, tomorrow's rainfall is read from the free Open-Meteo forecast API. Enter an IMD forecast in the controls to override it.

      How to read the outputs

      • Shortfall risk: probability that tomorrow's output falls below 90% of plan. Low under 30%, Medium 30 to 60%, High above 60%.
      • Why this rating: the features pushing risk up or down, from the model's own decision paths.
      • Recommended actions: the best combination of five actions within a cost budget of 3.0, judged by the what-if simulator. Costs and effects are editable assumptions to be agreed with mine planners.
      • Predictive maintenance: a linear trend of each machine's health gives hours to failure. It is a simple estimate and not a certified reliability model.
      • 3D reserve model: tonnage uses an assumed bulk density of 3.6 t/m³. The comparison with the hidden truth is only possible because the orebody is simulated.
